Diffu_HSI · residual warm-start · Phase 2a · 24 October 2026

Around the one-call regressor, the residual diffusion scores like a fixed-width Gaussian on CRPS, and a variance head beats both

Phase 2a asked whether a diffusion model centred on the one-call regressor gives calibrated per-pixel uncertainty and better texture than a regressor that predicts its own per-pixel variance. The main test ran at stride 8, where 63 of every 64 pixels are interpolated. The pre-registered verdict is no, and a second stride gives the same answer. The diffusion's CRPS is 10 to 11 % worse than the variance head's at both strides (9.8 % at stride 8, 10.9 % at stride 4, as a ratio of means). Its spread has almost the same width everywhere and is mostly white, high-frequency noise, so as a predictive distribution it does no better than one fixed-width Gaussian around the regressor. Three of the six pre-registered clauses turned out to be out of reach for a well-calibrated sampler (S2 only by chance); section 4 explains why, and the pre-registered verdict stands. The two calibration clauses were passable and failed on merit.

01The test, fixed before any number was seen

The spec of 22 October fixed six clauses at $d=8$ before training began. Calibration passes if C1 and C2 pass, the perceptual test passes if P1 and P2 pass, and "diffusion wins" needs both plus the sanity clause S1 and the validity clause S2. The $d=4$ run uses the same code and reports the same clauses without a gate.

What was trained

Each stride needed two half-data regressors for the cross-fitted centres, the residual diffusion and the variance head; $d=8$ also got a second-seed regressor for an ensemble baseline. Selection followed the pre-registered rule, the epoch with the lowest monitoring loss, on the 800 validation files that never include a gate cube.

02The d = 8 verdict: the variance head is the better uncertainty model

The single pre-registered verdict, taken once on 23 October at 18:44, fails five of six clauses. The variance head has the lower CRPS on 193 of the 200 cubes, and its per-pixel width ranks the errors with half the diffusion's AUSE (0.18 against 0.37). Of the pre-registered comparators the diffusion beats only the two-member regressor ensemble, whose spread is far too narrow to be useful; it ties the fixed-width Gaussian (−0.015 ± 0.010).

VERIFIED BEFORE IT WAS WRITTEN UP Six independent checks ran on the record before this page existed. Two recomputations, one from scratch in numpy and one as a 5000-draw bootstrap over the 113 source images, reproduce every clause bit for bit. Calibration, the perceptual test and "wins" pass in none of the 5000 draws. Every checkpoint, the centre cache, the prior and the split re-hash to the record, and the epochs are the ones the selection rule picks. The scripts and their structured result are in docs/analysis/phase2a_verify_d8/.

03Why: the spread has almost the same width everywhere, and it is noise

A good per-pixel uncertainty is narrow where the error is small and wide where it is large. The variance head behaves that way. The diffusion's spread barely changes: across cubes its log width rises with log error at slope 0.14 at $d=8$ and 0.07 at $d=4$, against 0.72 and 0.82 for the variance head. Across cubes the diffusion's log predicted variance correlates with the log squared error at 0.59 at $d=8$ and 0.48 at $d=4$, against 0.83 and 0.90 for the variance head. Pooled over the cubes, its predicted variance is only 0.71 of its squared error at $d=8$ (0.65 at $d=4$), although the mean of the per-cube spread ratios is 1.05 (0.93): a near-constant width is too wide on easy cubes and too narrow on hard ones.

The model does use its condition, but only to shape the spread. On the 8 stored $d=8$ cubes, under a swapped condition its spread map follows the donor cube (Spearman 0.55 with the donor, 0.25 with its own). The residual's conditional mean is close to zero by construction, so the condition has little else to do. On one cube, more sampling steps did not help: DDIM-50 and DDPM-1000 give a slightly worse CRPS than DDIM-10, with the same white deviations.

04Three clauses no calibrated sampler could pass, and one pass that means nothing

The verification found a flaw in the spec I wrote. P2 and S1 at $K=32$ cannot be passed by any sampler whose spread matches its error, and S2 has too little power to pass except by chance, so "diffusion wins" was unreachable at either stride, whatever the model. P1 passed for a reason unrelated to texture. C1 and C2 were passable; they are the result.

S1 MEASURED MONTE CARLO NOISE, NOT DRIFT The mean of $K$ draws carries the extra error $v/K$ from the spread $v$. A perfectly centred sampler with this spread is expected to show S1 = +0.058 at $K=32$; the run showed +0.052. Removing that term after the fact gives −0.006 ± 0.004 at $d=8$ and +0.001 ± 0.001 at $d=4$, both inside the rule's 2 SE; the pre-registered S1 still fails at both strides. The fix round confirmed the $1/K$ term: at $K=64$ S1 halved to +0.025. As written, S1 would need $K\approx 256$ at $d=8$ for a perfectly centred sampler.
S2 HAD NO POWER IN THIS DESIGN The samples are always added to the true centre, and the residual's conditional mean is near zero, so swapping the condition only changes the size of the $1/K$ noise term. Even an oracle-calibrated sampler would score +0.024 ± 0.019 at $d=8$ and fail in expectation. S2's failure says nothing about whether the condition is used; the probes in section 3 show it is.

05The pre-registered fix round: the K = 64 predictions, written first, all held

The spec allows one fix round when calibration fails: $K=64$ on the gate, and DDPM-1000 on the 50-cube DDPM subset. The predictions below were written into the results note before the run. P1 and P2 repeat the $K=32$ values by construction (the single sample is draw 0 with the same seed), so the informative predictions are C1, C2 and S1. The DDPM-1000 row's prediction (CRPS no better than DDIM-10, spread at least as wide and still white) is tested when its record lands.

Under the spec, part (a) has failed at $d=8$.

06The d = 4 contrast: smaller in absolute terms, not in relative ones

The spec predicted smaller gaps at $d=4$, where the posterior is narrower. In absolute units the CRPS gap halves, but so does the error itself. Scaled by the variance head's CRPS the gap does not shrink: 9.8 % at $d=8$ and 10.9 % at $d=4$ as a ratio of means, 12.4 % and 11.9 % as a mean of per-cube ratios. The diffusion now loses on all 200 cubes. The same white, nearly fixed-width spread appears, if anything stronger. Two strides are only two points, and they also differ in regressor, $\sigma_R$ and the roughness of the true residual, so none of these changes can be attributed to the stride alone.

Two findings change the picture at $d=4$. The variance head becomes nearly calibrated, with cov50 0.537 and cov90 0.897, inside both C2 bands. And comparing the regressor-centred diffusion with the linear-centred B separates centre from spread: the new diffusion has the lower CRPS (−0.058 ± 0.004) because its centre is better, while B's spread ranks the errors better, on 197 of 200 cubes.

08Rulings and compute

Decisions taken during execution that changed the spec, each recorded with its reason before it took effect. The last two items are different: findings made after the verdict, which change nothing in it.

  • K = 32 at both strides. Empirical coverage uses Weibull quantile positions, which give the nominal level for a calibrated sampler only when $K\ge 19$ at the 90 % level. The spec's $K=16$ at $d=4$ was withdrawn.
  • Provenance checks. A half regressor records the hash of the split it was trained on. The cache builder refuses a regressor whose residual scale or prior differs from the data's. The evaluator refuses a checkpoint sampled under the wrong parameterisation.
  • Stored figure cubes come from the class-stratified y-swap batch, not the first gate files.
  • The DDPM-1000 fix-round arm runs report-only at K = 8 on the 50-cube subset. The verdict refuses $K<19$, and a $K\ge19$ DDPM run would cost about 40 GPU-hours for a gate it cannot plausibly change.
  • Found after the verdict: a spec design flaw. P2 and S1 at $K=32$ could not be passed by a calibrated sampler, and S2 had too little power (section 4). They are recorded as failures, as pre-registered, and explained here.
  • Found by the final code review: noise seeded per batch, not per cube. Draw $k$ seeds its initial noise once per batch, so cubes at the same position in different batches share it, where the spec asked for a seed per cube. Records therefore reproduce bit for bit only at the same batch size; every record used 4. The review found that no clause could change: C1, P1, P2 and S1 miss or clear their thresholds by at least 4.8 cluster SE, C2's cov50 is far outside its band, and S2 compares cubes within one batch. A per-cube seed is deferred until after this phase's last record, so that all fix-round records share one scheme.
