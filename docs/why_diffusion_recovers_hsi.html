Why diffusion recovers the spectrum from 30 sensor channels

A snapshot camera gives us 30 numbers per pixel. We want 31 (or 160) spectral bands back. This note shows, with the actual sensor matrix and the actual data, why that is possible, why a conditional diffusion model is the right tool, why convolving along the spectral axis ("3-D diffusion") is the wrong tool, and where the experiments stand.

01The inverse problem looks almost square. It is not.

Per pixel the camera computes $y_p=R^{\mathsf T}x_p$: 30 weighted sums of the 31 bands. On paper only one direction of the spectrum is lost. In practice the 30 measurements are far from independent.

What a prior buys, measured

02Identifiability: on the spectral manifold, $y$ determines $x$

Natural spectra do not fill $\mathbb R^{31}$. Eight principal directions carry 99 % of their variance in HFD100, three in HASCID. The question is not whether 30 numbers determine 31, but whether they determine 8.

Proposition 1 · generic injectivity Let $\mathcal M\subset\mathbb R^{L}$ be compact with box-counting dimension $k$. If $N>2k$, then for Lebesgue-almost-every linear $A:\mathbb R^{L}\to\mathbb R^{N}$ the restriction $A|_{\mathcal M}$ is injective.
Proof. This is the embedology theorem of Sauer, Yorke and Casdagli (1991), the measure-theoretic form of Whitney's embedding theorem. $\square$

Here $N=30>2\cdot8=16$ for HFD and $30>2\cdot7=14$ for HASCID. The theorem covers generic matrices; for the actual $R^{\mathsf T}$ the certificate is the restricted conditioning on the principal subspace $V_k$ that carries the manifold,

$$\rho_k=\frac{\sigma_{\min}(R^{\mathsf T}V_k)}{\sigma_{\max}(R^{\mathsf T}V_k)},$$

because $\rho_k>0$ makes $R^{\mathsf T}$ bi-Lipschitz on $\operatorname{span}V_k\supset\mathcal M$ with inverse constant $1/\rho_k$, which implies injectivity on $\mathcal M$ itself.

Corollary 2 · the posterior is a point Under a prior supported on $\mathcal M$, the noiseless posterior $p(x\mid y)\propto p(x)\,\delta(y-R^{\mathsf T}x)$ is supported on $\mathcal M\cap(x^\star+\ker R^{\mathsf T})$, which by Proposition 1 is the single point $x^\star$. So $x=g(y)$ for a nonlinear inverse $g$ defined on $R^{\mathsf T}(\mathcal M)$. With measurement noise $\sigma$ the posterior spreads by about $\sigma/\rho_k$ along the $k$ manifold directions and not at all elsewhere, unlike the Gaussian posterior of §1, which spreads over all $L-11$ poorly measured directions.

That is the whole reason the task is possible: 30 channels do not determine 31 numbers, but they determine the 8 numbers that spectra actually have. A method succeeds to the degree that it knows $\mathcal M$ and can apply the dense, wavelength-specific operator that maps $y$ back onto it.

03Why diffusion computes exactly this posterior

Notation follows model/diffusion_trainer.py: $\bar\alpha_t=\prod_{s\le t}(1-\beta_s)$, $x_t=\sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}\,x_0+\sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}\,\varepsilon$ with $\varepsilon\sim\mathcal N(0,I)$ and $t$ uniform on $\{0,\dots,999\}$.

Proposition 3 · the trained network is the conditional score The minimiser over all measurable functions of $$\mathcal L=\mathbb E_{t,x_0,y,\varepsilon}\;w(t)\,\big\|\varepsilon-\varepsilon_\theta(x_t,y,t)\big\|_2^2,$$ for any positive weighting $w(t)$ (uniform, or min-SNR-$\gamma$), is $\varepsilon^\star(x_t,y,t)=\mathbb E[\varepsilon\mid x_t,y]$, and $$\nabla_{x_t}\log p_t(x_t\mid y)=-\frac{\varepsilon^\star(x_t,y,t)}{\sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}}.\tag{3.1}$$
Proof. The minimiser is pointwise in $(x_t,y,t)$, so a weight that depends on $t$ alone does not move it, and the pointwise $L_2$ minimiser is the conditional expectation. For (3.1), write $p_t(x_t\mid y)=\int\mathcal N(x_t;\sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}x_0,(1-\bar\alpha_t)I)\,p(x_0\mid y)\,dx_0$ and differentiate under the integral: $\nabla\log p_t=-\mathbb E[(x_t-\sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}x_0)\mid x_t,y]/(1-\bar\alpha_t)$, which is Tweedie's formula (Efron 2011). $\square$
$v$-prediction. The FSDP runs regress $v=\sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}\,\varepsilon-\sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}\,x_0$. Given $x_t$, $v$ is an invertible affine function of $\varepsilon$, so $\mathbb E[v\mid x_t,y]$ gives the same score; it only improves the conditioning of the regression at the two ends of the noise schedule (Salimans and Ho 2022), which is why it trains more stably in bf16.
$L_1$ loss. The repository minimises $\mathbb E|\varepsilon-\varepsilon_\theta|$, whose minimiser is the coordinate-wise conditional median. It equals the mean when the conditional law is symmetric, which holds at small $t$ where the posterior is unimodal, and it is a common robustness choice; strictly, the sampler below assumes the mean. Worth an ablation (§7), not a flaw in the argument.
Proposition 4 · sampling the posterior Let $s_\theta\approx\nabla\log p_t(x_t\mid y)$ with $\mathbb E\|s_\theta-\nabla\log p_t\|^2\le\varepsilon_{\text{score}}^2$. The reverse-time process driven by $s_\theta$ (Anderson 1982; Song et al. 2021), discretised as the DDPM or DDIM sampler in DiffusionTrainer.sample, returns a distribution $\hat p$ with $$\mathrm{TV}\big(\hat p,\;p(\cdot\mid y)\big)\le C\big(\varepsilon_{\text{score}}\sqrt{T}+\text{discretisation}+e^{-T}\big)$$ for any conditional data distribution with finite second moment; no log-concavity, smoothness or full support is needed (Chen, Lee, Lu et al. 2023, with early stopping for manifold-supported data).
Theorem 5 · reconstruction Combine Corollary 2 with Propositions 3 and 4: a conditional diffusion model trained to small loss on pairs $(x,\,y=R^{\mathsf T}x)$ and sampled with the reverse process returns samples concentrated at $g(y)=x^\star$. The error has two parts: the posterior spread allowed by $\rho_k$ and the measurement precision, and the score and discretisation error. In the noiseless, perfectly trained limit the sample is $x^\star$. $\square$

What the score must contain

Why not a plain regressor $y\mapsto x$? An $L_2$ regressor learns $\mathbb E[x\mid y]$. Where the posterior is multimodal (folds of $\mathcal M$ seen through $R^{\mathsf T}$, or unmeasured pixels when $d>1$) that mean lies off $\mathcal M$: a blurred, physically impossible spectrum. Diffusion samples the posterior and stays on $\mathcal M$; in the identifiable regime both agree, but diffusion regularises the ill-conditioned directions of §1 adaptively instead of with a fixed ridge, and repeated samples give uncertainty for free.

04Why convolving along the spectral or channel axis is the wrong hypothesis class

Two things are called "3-D" here. (a) The textbook idea: treat the cube as a one-channel volume $[1,L,H,W]$ and slide 3-D kernels over $(\lambda,h,w)$. (b) What this repository runs with --use_channel_3d_conv: inside the U-Net, every $3\times3$ convolution between $C$ feature channels is replaced by ChannelSpatialConv, which slides $K$ kernels of size $(k_c,3,3)$ over the $(c,h,w)$ volume and mixes the $K$ outputs with a $1\times1\times1$ convolution. One lemma covers both.

Case (a): a volume over wavelength. The presumed symmetry is false.

A stack of 3-D convolutions, point-wise nonlinearities and shared-weight pooling along $\lambda$ is equivariant to spectral shifts $S_\lambda$ (Cohen and Welling 2016). The best such network can do is the shift-average $\Pi D^\star$ of the Bayes denoiser $D^\star=\mathbb E[x_0\mid x_t,y]$; the error $\|D^\star-\Pi D^\star\|$ is irreducible. Three facts make it large.

The third fact is that the condition cannot even be placed on the $\lambda$ axis: $y_p\in\mathbb R^{30}$ while $x_p\in\mathbb R^{31}$, $\mathbb R^{64}$ or $\mathbb R^{160}$, and the 30 sensor channels are a uniformly thinned list of broadband filters with no metric on their index. Any lifting of $y$ into the volume needs a learned dense $30\to L$ map, which is a $1\times1$ channel-mixing operator, the 2-D ingredient. A purely 3-D conditional model is either ill-defined or a 2-D-channel model in disguise.

What a real 3-D network can do about it. Zero padding leaks absolute position near the borders (Islam, Jia and Bruce 2020; Kayhan and van Gemert 2020), so a deep 3-D network is only approximately equivariant and could in principle infer wavelength from its distance to the spectral edge, through many layers and without guarantee. Adding $\lambda$-positional encodings removes the equivariance and concedes the point: the spectral axis is then treated as a labelled channel axis at $L$-fold cost. The honest statement is that the inductive bias of 3-D convolution is the negation of the structure that makes the problem solvable; whatever such a model achieves, it achieves by undoing that bias.

Case (b): what --use_channel_3d_conv actually does

In ConvBlock2D_ChannelAware the convolved axis is not wavelength but the feature-channel axis of the U-Net; the 64 bands were already mixed densely by the $1\times1$ input_proj. Apply Lemma 4.1 with one volume channel: ChannelSpatialConv on a $C$-channel map is exactly a $3\times3$ Conv2d$(C\to C)$ whose weights are

$$W[c',c,\delta_h,\delta_w]=\sum_{k=1}^{K}m_k\,w_k[c'-c,\delta_h,\delta_w]\cdot\mathbf 1_{|c'-c|\le\lfloor k_c/2\rfloor},\tag{4.2}$$

a Toeplitz band of width $k_c$ generated by $K$ kernels.

  • It is a strict restriction of the block it replaces. Three to four orders of magnitude fewer channel-mixing weights per convolution. The design spec's statement that $K>1$ "multiplies the block's expressivity" is true relative to $K=1$; relative to the dense block it replaced, the variant is a severe cut. Everything in (3.3) that needs arbitrary channel mixing now has to be carried by the surviving dense layers: input_proj and final, the $1\times1$ up/down projections (present only when $C_{\text{in}}\neq C_{\text{out}}$), and the attention and feed-forward layers.
  • The convolved axis has no metric. Feature-channel indices of a freshly initialised network are exchangeable: permuting them, with the matching permutation in the next layer, leaves the function unchanged. "Channel $c$ is related to $c\pm1$ but not to $c\pm10$" is not a property of the data; the network must create a smooth channel ordering during training before the kernel is useful. The same holds for the sensor index $j$. This is weaker than the false-symmetry argument of case (a), since nothing is misrepresented, but the constraint buys no true prior knowledge in exchange for the capacity it removes.
  • Prediction. Because $\mathcal F_{\text{3D}}\subsetneq\mathcal F_{\text{2D}}$ at equal width, the variant cannot out-represent the plain block. Any empirical gain is regularisation and should vanish or reverse once the dense block is trained to convergence with comparable regularisation. The clean experiment is an A/B at equal parameter count, not equal width.
Cost, for case (a). Activations of a $\lambda$-volume model are $L$ times those of the 2-D model at equal width: the first stage of U2NetHyperspectral at base_channels=128 holds $128\times64\times64$ values per sample; the volume version holds $64\times$ more. The 2-D model already uses 16 GB at batch 3 on a 20 GB GPU. Case (b) keeps $[B,C,H,W]$ activations, which is why it was the variant that could be trained; it pays in capacity instead.

05Why 2-D spatial + 1-D spectral is right, and why the two-stage pipeline loses nothing

The two axes of the cube have opposite symmetry with respect to the measurement. The factorised design gives each axis the bias that is actually true for it.

5.1 The 1-D per-pixel model is already a consistent estimator

The likelihood factorises over pixels, so the posterior with the marginal spectral prior, $p(x_p\mid y_p)\propto p(x_p)\,\delta(y_p-R^{\mathsf T}x_p)$, is well defined and by Corollary 2 is a point mass at the true spectrum when $d=1$. The 1-D model (main_1d.py, U2Net1D, condition injected by cross-attention) learns exactly this posterior, and every pixel is an independent training sample: $40{,}811\times4{,}096\approx1.7\times10^{8}$ spectra in 31 dimensions instead of $4\times10^{4}$ cubes in $1.3\times10^{5}$. Density-estimation rates scale with the intrinsic dimension, $n^{-O(1/k)}$, so this is the statistically cheapest way to learn $\mathcal M$. It ignores the spatial prior, which costs only the residual spread of §2, and it cannot help at all when $d>1$.

5.2 The 2-D model adds the spatial prior where it is needed

With --ds 2 three quarters of the pixels have no measurement. Per-pixel identifiability fails for them and the prior term of (3.2) must fill them from their neighbours. The spatial prior is rich: bilinear interpolation of a stride-2 cube is already within 6.4 % of the original, and the model's cross-attention to the bilinearly resized context (create_context_for_resolution) is a learned, spectrum-aware version of that fill-in. The spectral axis stays a dense channel operator, so every measured pixel still gets the exact operator (3.3). The 2-D model is the smallest architecture that is correct for both $d=1$ and $d>1$.

5.3 The two-stage pipeline $Y_d\to Y\to X$ is exact posterior sampling

The sensor-SR model samples $Y\sim p(Y\mid Y_d)$; the HSI model then samples $X\sim p(X\mid Y)$. Because $Y_d=\mathcal D_d(Y)$ is a deterministic function of $Y$, $X$ is conditionally independent of $Y_d$ given $Y$, so

$$p(X\mid Y_d)=\int p(X\mid Y)\,p(Y\mid Y_d)\,dY,\tag{5.1}$$

and running the two stages in sequence is ancestral sampling from the joint posterior $p(X,Y\mid Y_d)$: the factorisation itself loses nothing. With approximate stages the errors add, by the triangle and data-processing inequalities for total variation:

$$\mathrm{TV}\big(\hat p(X\mid Y_d),\,p(X\mid Y_d)\big)\le\mathrm{TV}\big(\hat p(Y\mid Y_d),\,p(Y\mid Y_d)\big)+\mathbb E_{Y\mid Y_d}\,\mathrm{TV}\big(\hat p(X\mid Y),\,p(X\mid Y)\big),\tag{5.2}$$

each term controlled by Proposition 4. The first stage is well posed for two reasons: each pixel of $Y$ lies on $R^{\mathsf T}(\mathcal M)$, a 3-dimensional set (§2), so sensor-domain SR is spatial super-resolution of a 30-channel image with a 3-dimensional per-pixel manifold, the regime where conditional diffusion SR is known to work (Saharia et al. 2022); and the spatial prior is strong enough that even a trivial fill-in is close:

Why split at all instead of conditioning the HSI model on $Y_d$ directly? Both are valid: Theorem 5 applies to either conditioning. The split moves the spatial fill-in into a model whose target has 30 channels and a 3-dimensional per-pixel manifold instead of 64 channels and an 8-dimensional one, and lets the HSI stage be trained once, at $d=1$, where per-pixel identifiability holds. The price is the second term of (5.2): the HSI stage sees sampled $Y$, so it should be trained or fine-tuned with noise on $Y$ matched to the SR residual.

Two generations of runs exist. The single-GPU runs on this machine are fully logged; the FSDP runs on the SLURM cluster are known only through what the repository records about them.

HFD100, R_Device1, 64 interpolated bands, base_channels 128, batch 3, AdamW $10^{-4}$ cosine to $10^{-5}$, fp16 AMP. Read through §2–3: for a perfectly identifiable problem the $\varepsilon$-loss can reach zero at every $t$, since $\varepsilon=(x_t-\sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}\,g(y))/\sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}$ once $g(y)$ is known. A floor of 0.151 is therefore the sum of the posterior spread that precision and $\rho_k$ leave, the 1 % of spectral energy outside the well-measured subspace, and model capacity. The stride-2 floor is higher, as §5.2 predicts. The flat 910 epochs on the subset are a capacity or optimisation plateau, not a data limit. The 1-D HASCID floor of 0.026 fits a 3–7-dimensional manifold seen by 13 well-conditioned channels with $10^{8}$ training spectra. Both surviving checkpoints of the 2-D runs are unusable (checkpoint_epoch_911.pth truncated by a failed save, checkpoint_epoch_811.pth empty), so no PSNR or SAM exists for them.

Two GPUs, bf16, min-SNR-$\gamma=5$ weighting, $L_1$, FiLM time conditioning in the conv blocks, lr $3$–$5\times10^{-5}$ cosine to about $10^{-6}$, --ds_warmup_epochs 20 (20 epochs at $d=1$, then $d=2$), base_channels 256–512, --use_channel_3d_conv --channel_num_filters 4 --channel_kernel 7.

07Conclusions and what to measure next

  • Reconstruction is possible because spectra are 8-dimensional, not 31-dimensional. The 30 channels of R_Device1 carry about 11 well-conditioned directions, and those hold 98 % of the spectral variance. On the 8-dimensional principal subspace the measurement is injective with condition number about 30 (§1–2).
  • Conditional diffusion is the right estimator. The $\varepsilon$ or $v$ regression learns the conditional score exactly, the reverse process samples the posterior with a bound that needs no smoothness, and that posterior is a point when the problem is identifiable (§3). A regressor would blur multimodal cases; a linear prior is 5–14 % off.
  • Convolving along the spectral or channel axis removes capacity the problem needs. A 3-D convolution is a Toeplitz-banded channel mixer; over wavelength it imposes a symmetry that is 59 % false for this device, over feature channels it cuts the mixing weights by three to four orders of magnitude on an axis with no metric (§4).
  • The 2-D + 1-D factorisation and the two-stage pipeline are exact in the right sense. Spatial shifts commute with the measurement and spectral shifts do not, so shared weights belong in $(h,w)$ and dense mixing in $\lambda$. Sampling $Y$ then $X$ is ancestral sampling from the joint posterior (§5).
  • What is still missing is a reconstruction metric. The single-GPU runs plateaued at $\varepsilon$-$L_1$ 0.151 ($d=1$) and 0.162 ($d=2$) with no usable checkpoint; the cluster runs have no committed results (§6).

Next steps that follow from this note

  1. Evaluate against the free baselines. HSI stage: relative RMSE and SAM on the test split versus the Gaussian-prior MMSE of §1 (5–14 %). Sensor-SR stage: versus the bilinear columns of §5 (4.4 % at $d_{sr}=2$ to 17.9 % at $d_{sr}=8$). A model that does not beat these has learned nothing beyond second-order statistics.
  2. A/B the ChannelSpatialConv variant against the dense block at equal parameter count. Lemma 4.1 predicts the dense block wins or ties at convergence; if the variant wins, the gain is regularisation and should be reproducible with weight decay on the dense block.
  3. Confirm the device ordering Device1 ≳ Device2 ≫ PH5 at equal training; a cheap test of the identifiability analysis.
  4. Reconstruct 31 bands and interpolate to 64 afterwards. The 31→64 map (expand_wavelens) has rank 31, so the 64-band target lives on a 31-dimensional subspace the network currently has to learn.
  5. Two-stage robustness, equation (5.2): train or fine-tune the HSI stage on sampled $Y$ from the SR stage, or with matched noise on $Y$.
  6. HASCID: 10.7 % of the measurement energy comes from bands 161–204 that are not part of the 160-band target. Reconstruct all 204 bands or compute $y$ from the first 160 only; otherwise those bands act as structured noise.
  7. Housekeeping. Atomic checkpoint saves (write to a temporary name, then os.replace); the legacy runs lost both final checkpoints to interrupted writes. In the legacy fp16 path gradient clipping is applied to scaled gradients (clip_grad_norm_ without scaler.unscale_); the FSDP path is correct. Ablate $L_1$ against $L_2$.
