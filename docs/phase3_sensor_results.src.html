Phase 3 — Reconstruction from a poor EC sensor

The paper's claim is that a diffusion model is the most suitable way to recover the spectrum seen by a poor electrochromic (EC) sensor: 30 voltage states with broad, overlapping responses, of which only about carry independent information about the 31 bands. Phase 3 tests that claim where the sensor's poorness matters. E1 adds measurement noise to Device 1 in sequential mode and sweeps the SNR from the 60 dB floor down to 20 dB. E2 applies models trained on Device 1, without retraining, to Device 2 and to PH5. E3 runs Device 1 in snapshot (mosaic) mode, where each pixel measures one state. Two diffusion lines compete against the noise-aware linear MMSE (L), a one-call regressor (D) and a variance head (V): B, an unconditional spectral–spatial prior guided by the measurement (ΠGDM), and C, a conditional residual diffusion around the MMSE estimate, trained over the whole noise and mode family.

The test was fixed in advance. The user approved the spec on 4 November 2026, and the thresholds of clauses T1–T4 were written into scripts/sensor/sd_verdict.py before any record existed. Every number on this page is read from the evaluation records at build time.

01The sensor, and what the linear inverse can do with it

The 30 normalised columns of Device 1's response overlap heavily. of its 30 singular values are below 1 % of the largest (condition number ), so noise in those directions is amplified without bound and only a spectral prior can fill them in. The paper's main figure. The regressor is the most accurate method at every SNR, and its lead grows as the noise rises. Clause T1 asked whether the better diffusion line's mean-of-32 RMSE stays within 5 % of the regressor's, by 2 clustered SE.

  • For the point estimate, the regressor is the right tool. D is and halves the MMSE error at 20 dB ( against %). C's posterior mean is level with D only at the high-SNR end. The spec predicted C would pass T1 everywhere, and that prediction was wrong at low SNR.

D, V and C were trained on Device 1 only. Clause T3 asked whether a line's error ratio to the MMSE on Device 2 at 30 dB stays within 10 % of its ratio on Device 1. PH5 is report-only.

PH5's response has rank and a very different posterior-std structure, so its $(\hat x, s)$ maps lie outside every family model's training distribution. On this sensor the paper's “no retraining” claim is false for the conditional networks. B's PH5 rows were dropped for cost.

05Snapshot mode (E3)

In snapshot mode each pixel measures one of the 30 states in a 5 × 6 tile. The exact per-pixel posterior from one channel is essentially the prior mean (– %). The classical baseline demosaics the 30 channels first and then runs the sequential MMSE. Clause T4 asks for a 15 % improvement over that baseline at 30 dB, i.e. RMSE at most % against %.

Why C loses here. In snapshot mode the centre $\hat x$ is the one-channel posterior, so the whitened residual is large and highly structured: modelling it is the demosaicing problem. The one-call regressor learns it (– %). The diffusion trained on the same target does not: C1 is – %, and its samples are heavily over-dispersed (cov50 –, cov90 ≥ ). The variance head inherits the poor centre and cannot shrink enough to be calibrated (cov50 at 30 dB). A diffusion centred on D's prediction, Phase 2a's construction, would have been the fairer snapshot test. That is out of scope here and is recorded for the write-up.

06Line B: a prior that first had to learn to be one

The first B records were degenerate, and the cause was the prior, not the sampler. On the isotropic Gaussian toy the ΠGDM code reproduces the exact posterior at 50 steps. With a per-pixel Wiener filter as the denoiser the same code reconstructs real cubes close to the MMSE. The learned v-prediction prior, though, did not read its input at low and medium noise.

07The pre-registered clauses

One row per clause and setting, for both diffusion lines, as the verdict file reports them. The main-line rule names the line that passes more of T1–T4, with T2 at 20 dB breaking a tie.

08Runs and budget

Five 100-epoch-class trainings at base 64 on one RTX A4500 each, and three 10-epoch prior pilots. Selection followed the pre-registered rule: the lowest monitoring loss for D and V, and the fixed epoch 100 for the diffusions.

09Deviations and rulings

  • ΠGDM's correction coefficient. The draft's $\sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}$ diverged on the isotropic toy. The correction is now $\hat x_0 + (1-\bar\alpha_t)/\sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}\cdot g$, the score correction in $x_0$ coordinates.
  • Noise seeds per file. Each cube's noise seed is the crc32 of its path relative to the data root, not its list position. Records on the 50 and the 200 cubes therefore see identical noise and pair by file.
  • K = 32 everywhere; B on the 50-cube subset. Coverage verdicts need K ≥ 19, so the draft's K = 16 was withdrawn. B's ΠGDM cost limits it to the 50-cube subset, paired against the 200-cube records on the common files.
  • B's prior retrained with x₀-prediction. This amends spec §5.5's “v-prediction” for the prior only (section 6). The pilot met the pre-registered decision rule (monotone, and at least 30 % below the current prior at t ≤ 300). C stays v-prediction, and the harness builds B's sampler from the checkpoint's own prediction type.
  • B's cost. A B record costs ≈ GPU-h, not the plan's 0.63. The 12 GPU-h cap was exceeded with the user's GPUs otherwise idle.
  • PH5's B rows dropped. First in the spec's drop order once B's cost was known; PH5 is report-only anyway.
