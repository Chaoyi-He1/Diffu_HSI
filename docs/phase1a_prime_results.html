Diffu_HSI · residual warm-start · Phase 1a′ · 16 October 2026

A one-step regressor beats the baseline by 8.9 %. The target needs 15 %.

Phase 1a left two questions open: what B's sampling chain does, and whether a plain one-step regressor with B's architecture can beat B's one-step read-out. Both are answered. The chain is centred closer to the truth than B's own one-step output, and the L1 regressor beats the baseline, B, and B's 8-draw mean in a single call. Neither regressor reaches the 1.9985 % target, so the gate does not license the full-size run. Both overfit by epoch 10–15; on the monitoring split, which is not the gate, that accounts for most of the missing gain.

01The question Phase 1a left, and how it was gated

Phase 1a found that B's best read-out is a single DDIM step: every extra sampling step made the result worse. That raised a plain question. If one call at $t=999$ is all B uses, what does a network trained only for that call do?

What was run

  1. Step 0, diagnostics of B. Sample B's chain $K$ times per cube with the harness's own seeding: DDIM-10 and the one-step call with $K=8$ on the 200 cubes, DDPM-1000 with $K=4$ on the 50 DDPM cubes. From the draws, estimate each chain's centre and spread per cube. Draws 0 and 1 reproduce B's stored evaluation to $10^{-6}$, so the diagnostics sample exactly the chain Phase 1a scored.
  2. Step 1, two regressors. Train B's architecture (58.9 M parameters, base 64) from scratch with B's budget (batch 8, 100 epochs, lr $10^{-4}$ cosine, seed 42) and a direct loss on $r$ at $t=999$ with a zero $x_t$: one run with L1, one with L2. 7.6 h and 6.9 h on one GPU each.
  3. Gate. On the 200 held-out cubes, paired per cube: a regressor passes if it beats the baseline and B's ddim1 by more than 2 SE and is valid under the proposed y-swap rule. The target for the full-size run is 15 % below the baseline, 1.9985 %. A second training seed runs only if the best regressor meets the target. Final checkpoints only, as pre-registered.
Six deviations from the spec were approved with the plan. The binding validity check is the proposed one-step rule (at $t=999$ the spec's 0.9 ratio needs a one-step gain of ≈ 5.4 % for a calibrated model and ≈ 8 % for one with B's shrinkage, so B's 4.4–4.6 % cannot pass it); both losses are trained; the regressor reuses B's architecture; the diagnostics budget is fixed; seed 43 runs only on success; and no new driver was built. All six are listed in the plan's 'Deviations from the spec' section; the as-built block records how deviations 1, 2, 4 and 5 played out.

02Step 0: B's chain is centred closer to the truth than its one-step output

Phase 1a left two hypotheses. H0: the chain samples an unconditional residual, centred on $r=0$. H1: it is a posterior sampler centred on B's one-step estimate. Neither holds. The chain's long-run mean beats the one-step centre; what a single draw pays is the spread.

What step 0 settles B is a conditional model, and its chain is not a posterior sampler around its one-step estimate: $\lambda>1$ by many SE for both chains. The Phase 1a losses from sampling are the spread, paid on top of a centre that is better than the one-step read-out. Matching one step in mean RMSE by sampling the chain takes about 9 DDIM-10 draws (≈ 90 NFE); distilling the chain's mean into one call is option 7 in §6, a direction whose cost is not measured here.
Reading the numbers. $\lambda=(b^2-c_2)/(b^2-c_1)$ with $b$ the baseline's RMSE. The single-draw version of this ratio, which uses a draw's RMSE² in place of $c_1$, is biased upward: on the one-step row, whose true $\lambda$ is 1 by construction, it reads $1.272\pm0.025$. Phase 1a's $\rho^2\approx9\,\%$, its estimate of the share of the residual energy B's one-step output removes, came from such single-draw RMSEs; the noise-free one-step centre removes 11.4 % of the mean error energy.

03The regressors beat B's one-step read-out; only L1 also beats its 8-draw mean

Direct regression at $t=999$ changes three things at once: the objective (a full-weight loss at one $t$, where B's training weighted $t=999$ at $4.8\times10^{-5}$ of its peak), a single deterministic call, and a zero $x_t$ in place of noise. At 1 NFE, L1 scores 2.1422 % and L2 2.1829 %.

In error energy, L1 is level with B's chain centre. L1's mean error energy is 5.031 pct² against DDIM-10's centre 4.908 $(+0.12\pm0.09)$: one call reaches what B's chain reaches only in its $K\to\infty$ mean. It removes 17.3 % of the baseline's error energy (L2 13.4 %). On the 50 DDPM cubes, DDPM-1000's centre removes 22.8 %, DDIM-10's 18.7 % and L1 17.0 %. The target is 15 % in mean RMSE, which is about 28 % of the energy if every cube improves by the same factor.

04Validity: both regressors use their condition, and B does too

A model can beat the baseline by learning a cube-independent correction. The y-swap test gives each model another cube's condition and checks that its gain disappears. All three models pass the proposed rule; the regressors also pass the spec's 0.9 ratio.

05Both regressors overfit, so the gate's numbers are epoch-100 lower bounds

Each run's training loss falls for 100 epochs while its monitoring loss, on 800 validation files the gate never sees, is lowest at epoch 15 (L1) and 10 (L2), is higher than that minimum at every later check, and rises at every check from epoch 35 (L1) and 60 (L2). B's monitoring loss fell monotonically in Phase 1a.

How far the inference carries The gate's 17.3 % and 13.4 % come from the epoch-100 checkpoints. They are lower bounds set by overfitting; they do not measure what $(\hat x_0, y_4)$ can predict. The transfer to the gate is approximate: the monitoring files are different patches from the same validation pool as the gate cubes, with the gate's class mix, and they include patches of all 113 gate source images. No epoch-25 checkpoint was ever scored on the gate, and this phase scored the final checkpoints as pre-registered. Any future checkpoint selection must use the monitoring split, and because that split shares source images with the gate, the selection rule has to be fixed before the gate is looked at.

06The decision

The gate selects the branch "best regressor PASS, target not met". go_1b is false and the seed-43 runs did not happen. Nothing is running or queued. The options below are the note's nine, in its order, each with its cost; the choice is yours.

  • 1. Stop early or select the checkpoint on the monitoring split, at base 64. This targets most of the gap: on the monitoring files the epoch-25 checkpoints remove 26.7 % / 23.7 % of the residual energy against 15.4 % / 10.7 % at epoch 100, and the mean-RMSE gain rises to 14.7 % (L1) against the 15 % target. Add weight decay (0 now) or augmentation if early stopping alone does not hold the minimum. Pre-register the selection rule (lowest monitoring loss on the 800 non-gate files) before any gate run.Costone full base-64 run is 6.9–7.6 h on one GPU; two variants fit side by side. The monitoring minima came after 1.3 h (L1) and 0.8 h (L2). The saved epoch-25 and 50 checkpoints could be scored on the gate in minutes on CPU, but only as a labelled post-hoc look.

The regressor at base 256 with FSDP at batch 8 per GPU: 16× the parameters (940 M against 58.9 M), trained on the same 9 000 files on which base 64 already overfits by epoch 10–15. 07A correction to Phase 1a, and what the phase cost

Correction · B's one-step y-swap had leaked the true residual Phase 1a's y-swap built B's input at $t=999$ as $$x_t=\sqrt{\bar\alpha_{999}}\,r+\sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_{999}}\,\varepsilon=0.00635\,r+0.99998\,\varepsilon,$$ which carries a trace of the true residual; B's real one-step call starts from pure noise. B read the leak: with the same $\varepsilon$ and $x_t=\varepsilon$, its true-condition score on the 32 y-swap cubes is 2.3128 instead of 2.2479 (leaky − leak-free $-0.0649\pm0.0102$, lower on 32 of 32 cubes), and the leaked term is the only difference between the two runs. The final code review found it; the fix adds a pure-noise option, re-ran B's y-swap, and makes the gate refuse the leaky record.
What changes. Phase 1a's "7.1 % gain on the y-swap cubes" is 4.4 %, consistent with the 200-cube 4.6 %; the cube selection did not matter. What holds. B passes the proposed rule with either record and fails the spec's 0.9 ratio with either (0.924 leak-free, against 0.917, the ratio a model whose swapped prediction is uncorrelated with the truth would show at a 4.4 % gain, $\sqrt{v/(2-v)}$ with $v=(1-0.044)^2$), so Phase 1a's conclusion that the 0.9 rule lacks power at this gain stands. The $t=100$ and $300$ checks are unaffected, since there the leaky construction is the sampler's own input distribution.
